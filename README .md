## eKhaya Connect: Entrepreneurial WIL Project

A community-owned wireless internet service provider (WISP) for Khayelitsha. My team started from a real problem, tested it with residents, and then planned, branded and prototyped the service using Agile practices and a Trello board.

**17**Khayelitsha households interviewed door to door

**14 / 17**said yes or maybe to paying for affordable internet

**R399**per month for 10 Mbps uncapped (launch package)

**16**backlog cards across 4 sprints on our Trello board

### The problem

Many households in Khayelitsha have no realistic home internet option because major providers have under-invested in infrastructure there. That limits access to education, job applications and online services.

### The business idea

An uncapped fixed-wireless home connection. A small dish at each home connects to a local wireless network that shares a high-speed fibre link. The 10 Mbps package is priced at about R399 a month, close to what many households already pay for satellite TV.

## 1. Agile Team Charter

A charter sets out a team's shared purpose, values, working agreements and boundaries. This one is compiled from our project documents and from how the team actually worked.

### Evidence
### Purpose and vision

**Purpose:** bring affordable, reliable home internet to Khayelitsha households and small businesses.

**Vision:** a community-owned WISP offering uncapped home Wi-Fi for the price households already pay for satellite TV. Our tagline is *Connecting Homes · Empowering Communities*.

**Core values** (from our brand guide): Affordable, Reliable and Local.

### Objectives

* Validate the problem with real households.
* Offer a 10 Mbps uncapped package at about R399 per month.
* Register residents and verify their addresses.
* Install and configure the wireless network, fibre link and home equipment.
* Provide payments, support and monitoring.
* Design a mobile and web experience, then test it against the mockups.

### Working agreements

* All work lives on the Trello board: Product backlog, then Sprint lists.
* Each user story has a checklist and each checklist item has a named owner.
* Short stand-ups use three questions: what did I complete, what will I do next, what is blocking me.

* A board walkthrough checks what is needed to advance the work, whether anyone is working off-board, what the team must finish and where the blockers are.
* Brand rules in the brand guide are followed in all materials: colours, typography and tone of voice.
* Group artefacts are credited to the team, and individual write-ups are done by each member.

### Definition of done

A card is done when every item on its checklist is ticked (for example 4/4), the owner of each item has completed it, and the card is moved to the right sprint list on Trello.

### Reflection
Writing this charter showed me how much of our team agreement was real but unwritten. We had a clear shared goal (affordable internet at about R399 a month), we used the same stand-up questions, and Trello made ownership visible. What we did not have was one signed document that everyone could point to.

The gaps show up in the evidence. Our logbook repeats "minor scheduling conflicts" and "time constraints", which a working agreement on meeting times could have reduced. Our project is also called *Khayelitsha Connect* on the logo and wireframes but *eKhaya Connect* in the brand guide and architecture diagram, which a charter line on naming would have prevented.

Next time I would write the charter in the first meeting, include meeting times, communication channels and decision-making, and revisit it at each sprint review so it stays a living document.

### Reflection
User stories kept us focused on the resident instead of on technology. Every story answers "so that…", and for a community service that matters: the benefit is access to education, work and online services, not just a working router.

The registration card taught me the most. Splitting one story into four checklist items, each with a named owner, made accountability clear and showed how the tasks depend on each other: the form has to exist before information is captured, and addresses must be verified before a confirmation is sent. My own task, verifying addresses, links directly to the coverage check in our architecture diagram.

Looking back, some cards were much larger than others (Website Development has seven checklist items, UI/UX Design six), so they were harder to estimate. If I did this again I would split large stories, add estimates, and write acceptance criteria that can be tested, not only checklist wording.

### Reflection
The backlog covers the whole service, not just software: surveys, towers and fibre sit next to payments, support and a website. That matched our idea of eKhaya Connect as a business and helped us see that the digital platform depends on the physical network, and the other way round.

What the board does not have is explicit priority, effort estimates or value. I ordered the items by dependency, but that is my analysis added afterwards, not something recorded in Trello. A stronger backlog would carry a priority and an estimate on every card, and would be reordered after each sprint review using resident feedback.

I also learned that a backlog is easier to defend when it links to evidence. Our interviews (14 of 17 households would or might pay, and 13 of 17 rated their frustration 4 or 5 out of 5) support the decision to put registration and the 10 Mbps package first.

**What the evidence does and does not show.** The Sprint 4 checklists were still at 0 when this screenshot was taken, although the design artefacts (wireframes, brand board) and the group website already exist. The board's status marks were therefore not always kept in step with the work. Dates are only recorded on two cards (Survey Installation Locations and Customer Support), so I have not invented dates for the other sprints.

### Reflection
Splitting the work into four sprints of four cards gave each sprint a clear theme: launch, operate, strengthen, then design the digital experience. It made the plan easy to explain and helped the team see what mattered first.

The board also taught me a lesson about honesty in tracking. Some cards are ticked as complete while their checklists show 0/4, and the Sprint 4 checklists were not updated although design work existed. If the board does not match reality, a reader cannot trust it. I would agree on a definition of done, update checklists the moment an item is finished, and hold a short sprint review at the end of each sprint to close cards properly.

Next time I would also add a sprint goal, start and end dates, and an estimate to each sprint list so that progress can be measured rather than described.

### Retrospective

#### What worked

* The same three stand-up questions every day kept updates short and comparable.
* The brand guide was started on 28 July and completed on 29 July.
* Nobody worked off the board.

#### What did not

* Scheduling conflicts and time constraints came up repeatedly.
* Waiting for team feedback slowed the prototype.
* Entries did not say who did which task.
* My daily detail stops after 31 July, so later progress is shown only by Trello dates and documents.

#### What I will change

* Fix meeting times at the start of the week.
* Name the owner of each completed item.
* Link each stand-up line to a Trello card.
* Keep daily entries going for the whole period, not only the first week.

### Reflection
Keeping the logbook turned the stand-up questions into a habit. Writing "what are my impediments" every day made problems visible early: on 29 July we were waiting for feedback, on 30 July we were short of time, and by 31 July the prototype review and documentation were finished.

The logbook period is longer than the daily entries I have. The stand-ups cover 28 to 31 July, and for the rest of the period up to 21 August the evidence is the Trello dates and project documents. That gap is a weakness I would fix by keeping the same three questions going every working day, because a logbook is only useful as evidence if it is continuous.

My entries are short, and they show the team's progress better than my individual contribution. Next time I would record the task I personally completed, link it to the Trello card, and note how long it took.

#### Connecting the whole portfolio

The five sections form one story. Interviews with 17 households gave us a validated problem. The charter set the purpose and ways of working. User stories turned that purpose into resident-focused tasks, the product backlog ordered them, and the sprint backlog scheduled them. The logbook shows the work happening day by day, and the brand board, wireframes, architecture diagram and website are the outputs.

The artefacts also show that our thinking changed over time. The logo and wireframes use the name *Khayelitsha Connect* and offer Starter, Family and Business packages (10, 20 and 50 Mbps), while the brand guide and architecture use *eKhaya Connect* and the group website lists Basic, Standard and Premium plans. These differences are the clearest lesson of the project: agree one name, one price list and one source of truth early, and record every change in the backlog.
